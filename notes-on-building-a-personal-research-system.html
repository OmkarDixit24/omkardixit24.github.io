Notes on Building a Personal Research System

A research system is only as good as the questions it helps you ask and the speed with which it resurfaces relevant material. Most systems fail because they optimise for capture rather than retrieval, or for neatness rather than use.

The core loop is simple: capture lightly, process deliberately, connect aggressively, and review on a cadence. Everything else is implementation detail.

Capture without friction

If capture is hard, you will not do it. Inbox-style capture—quick notes, highlights, links—should take seconds. The cost of imperfect capture is low. The cost of lost insight is high. Process later; capture now.

Processing is where structure appears. Turn raw captures into atomic notes with clear titles and a single idea each. Link them to related notes. The graph that forms over time is more valuable than any individual note.

A research system that you do not trust under pressure is not a system—it is a hobby.

Review is the neglected step. Without periodic review, the system becomes a graveyard of past thoughts. A weekly or monthly pass to resurface, prune, and re-link keeps the knowledge alive and usable when a live decision needs it.

Start smaller than feels serious. One reliable inbox, a handful of well-linked notes, and a fixed review habit beat an elaborate setup that collapses after three weeks.
