Eat the Frog!

Essay • July 2026

Mark Twain once famously remarked that if the first thing you do each morning is to eat a live frog, you can go through the day with the satisfaction of knowing that that is probably the worst thing that is going to happen to you all day.

"If it's your job to eat a frog, it's best to do it first thing in the morning. And If it's your job to eat two frogs, it's best to eat the biggest one first."

In his classic productivity framework, Brian Tracy transforms this metaphor into an essential operating principle for modern work. Your "frog" is your biggest, most critical task—the one you are most likely to procrastinate on if you don't do something about it immediately. It is also the single task that can have the greatest positive impact on your goals and momentum.

The friction most people experience throughout their workday isn't a lack of time, but a leak of cognitive energy. When you leave a complex, demanding task looming over your head while answering low-value emails or organizing minor spreadsheets, you carry a subconscious tax. Every hour spent delaying the core task drains willpower that could have been used to execute it with clarity.

By training yourself to tackle the hardest challenge immediately upon starting your routine, you break the cycle of active procrastination. You gain instant operational velocity, establishing a quiet psychological high ground that carries over into every secondary responsibility for the rest of the day.
