Reading as a Portfolio

Most people treat reading as consumption. A better frame is to treat it as a portfolio. You allocate attention across assets that have different expected returns, different time horizons, and different correlations with the rest of your thinking.

Some books are like index funds: broad, reliable, and compounding slowly. Others are concentrated bets—dense, difficult, and potentially transformative if they land. A healthy reading portfolio holds both, and rebalances deliberately.

Position sizing attention

Time is the scarce resource. Finishing every book you start is a form of over-concentration. Abandoning books that stop paying is not failure; it is portfolio management. The goal is not completion rate. The goal is the quality of the mental models you end up carrying.

Primary sources tend to have higher expected value than secondary summaries, but they also require more time and context. Mixing the two—using secondary work as a map and primary work as the territory—keeps the portfolio from becoming either shallow or impossibly slow.

What you choose not to read is as important as what you do. Opportunity cost applies to attention the same way it applies to capital.

The practical habit is simple: keep a short written thesis for why you are reading something, and revisit it halfway through. If the thesis no longer holds, exit the position. Your future self will thank you for the reclaimed hours.
