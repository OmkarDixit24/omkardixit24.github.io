The Quiet Edge of Primary Sources

Secondary sources are convenient. They summarise, frame, and package. Primary sources are slower and often less polished. The edge lives in the gap between the two.

When you read the original filing, the original letter, the original transcript, you see what the summariser chose to emphasise and what they left out. Those choices are not neutral. They reflect incentives, time constraints, and prior beliefs. Over a large enough sample, reading primary material changes the texture of your understanding.

Friction as feature

Primary sources have friction. That friction is part of the value. It forces you to do the interpretive work yourself instead of inheriting someone else’s frame. The cost is real—time, attention, occasional confusion. The return is independence of mind.

This does not mean abandoning secondary work. Good secondary analysis can point you toward the right primary documents and help you notice patterns you might miss. The mistake is stopping at the secondary layer and treating it as the final word.

The people who form independent views are usually the ones who are willing to sit with the unfiltered material longer than is comfortable.

In practice this looks like reading the 10-K before the sell-side note, the founder’s early interviews before the polished profile, the original paper before the tweet thread. Small habits. Large cumulative difference.
